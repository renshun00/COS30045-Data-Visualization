Technology Overview

How panel type affects energy use

🖥️

LED / LCD

The most common TV technology. A LED-backlit LCD panel is generally the most energy-efficient option per cm² of screen, particularly with local dimming. Typical power: 50–180 W depending on size.

✨

OLED

Each pixel produces its own light, delivering perfect blacks and vibrant colours. Energy draw depends heavily on screen brightness and content. Dark scenes use significantly less power than bright ones. Typical: 70–220 W.

⚡

QLED / Mini-LED

Quantum dot or Mini-LED backlights deliver brighter images with better HDR performance. They tend to consume more power at peak brightness than standard LED panels. Typical: 100–280 W.

Expert Tips

Reduce your TV's running costs

🔆

Lower the backlight

Reducing a TV's backlight or OLED brightness setting from maximum to 50% can cut power draw by 30–40% with little visible difference in most room lighting conditions.

🌙

Enable eco mode

Most modern televisions have an Eco or Energy Saving mode that automatically adjusts brightness based on ambient light. Enabling it can save 10–25% of power consumption over time.

🔌

Switch off at the wall

A TV on standby still draws 0.3–1.5 W. Over a year, that adds up to 2–14 kWh — small but completely avoidable. Smart power strips make this effortless.
